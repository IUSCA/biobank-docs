# Investigations

An investigation is one research question and the work done to answer it. It keeps the question's plan, the populations it studies, the results, and the conclusions in one place, with a full history of every change.

## What an investigation holds

- **A plan.** It states the question and how the work will answer it.
- **Segments.** A segment is a saved definition of a group of people. The segments are the investigation's population.
- **Queries.** A query describes a segment, for example as a count of people broken down by sex or age band.
- **Concept sets.** A concept set is a named list of codes, such as diagnosis or drug codes, that segments and queries use.
- **Records.** These are decision records, findings, notebooks, snapshots, the outcome, and linked publications.
- **Threads.** These hold the conversations with the assistant.

An investigation grants no access to data. Adding someone as a member does not let them see more than they could see already. Every result is a count or a summary, never a participant row or identifier.

## Opening Investigations

Choose **Investigations** in the sidebar. If it is not in your sidebar, investigations are not offered to your account on this portal yet.

### Start an investigation

1. Under **What question would you like to investigate?**, type your research question.
2. Set the **Assistant** switch. With it on, the assistant drafts a title, the plan's Population, Measures, and Approach, and a segment for each group of people the question needs. With it off, the investigation is named after your question and you build everything yourself.
3. With the assistant on, the **Search published work** switch lets the assistant search published literature for you. It is off unless you turn it on.
4. Press **Begin**. The investigation page opens.

Nothing the assistant drafts counts until you accept it. The **What happens next** card beside the form describes the steps for your choice.

### Example investigations

The **Example investigations** card opens a list of ready-made studies. Press **Open a copy** to make your own copy of one. Nothing you change in a copy reaches anyone else. Press **Open your copy** to return to it later, or **Start a fresh copy** to begin again.

### Find your investigations

**Your investigations** lists every investigation you own or belong to.

- **Current** and **Archived** choose which ones the list shows.
- The search box finds investigations by title, question, definition, decision, or member.
- **Mine** and **Shared with me** narrow the list by owner.
- **Needs you**, **Running**, and **Concluded** narrow it by state.

## The investigation page

The page has three columns.

- **Left:** the **Outline** tab lists the overview, the plan, each segment with its queries, the concept sets, and the records. The **Activity** tab lists recent saves and changes.
- **Middle:** the document itself. The overview and the plan come first, then each segment with its queries, then the records.
- **Right:** the conversation with the assistant.

The header shows the analysis release that new segments use, who else is on the page, the **Members** button, and the investigation's state. The state is **Active**, **Concluded**, or **Archived**. The milestone strip shows **Question**, **Segments**, **Approach**, **Findings**, **Request**, and **Concluded**. Select a milestone to narrow the **Open decisions** list to it.

## The plan

The plan has six sections: Question, Prior work, Population, Measures, Approach, and Open work.

- Click a section's line to read all of it.
- Click the pencil beside a section to edit it, then press **Save**.
- An empty section appears as a button after **Write a section:**. Click it to write that section.

Segment drafts from the assistant appear beside Population, each with **Accept** and **Decline**. Prior work can list leads from a literature search, each with **Add to prior work** and **Dismiss**.

## Segments, concept sets, and queries

These three kinds of definition are the investigation's working parts.

- **Segments.** Press **New segment**, or **Segment** in the outline, to start one. A segment is a tree of rules grouped as All of, Any of, or Not. Its block shows how many people it holds.
- **Concept sets.** Press the plus button beside **Concept sets**, above the document, to start one. A concept set has includes and excludes.
- **Queries.** Press **Query** under a segment to add a query that describes it. Each query card shows its result.

Each definition has a name in lowercase letters, digits, and underscores. When one definition names another, it follows that definition's newest saved revision. An edit therefore changes the results of everything that names it.

### Small counts

The portal hides small counts to protect privacy. A count below the portal's floor shows as "fewer than" followed by the floor. A zero shows as 0. A segment that small takes no queries, and its **Query** button says why.

### History and editing together

Every save writes a new numbered revision, and no revision is ever deleted. **History** on a definition lists its revisions. **Restore** writes an old revision's content as a new revision.

While you edit, **Check what this reaches** lists every definition your save would change. Members see who is viewing or editing each part of the page. If someone saves a definition while you edit it, your save is refused and you choose how to combine the two changes.

A save that breaks a definition depending on it is still saved. The broken definition gets a red outline, and the header counts broken definitions.

The ⋯ menu on a definition holds its other actions, such as **Rename…**, **Hide…**, and **Copy to another investigation…**.

## The assistant

The assistant works inside one investigation, in the right-hand column. It reads that investigation's plan, records, threads, and definitions, and nothing else. It works from counts and summaries. Everything it writes is advisory, so check its work before you rely on it.

- **Threads.** Each conversation is a thread. Every member sees every thread.
- **Naming a definition.** Type @ in a message to pick one of the investigation's definitions.
- **The Assistant switch.** Only the owner can turn the assistant on or off for the investigation. Switching it off stops any work in progress.

### Review and Auto

**Review** and **Auto** sit beside **Send** and decide what the assistant's next reply may change.

- **Review** is the default. The assistant's changes are drafts that only you can see. A draft has a dashed blue outline. Use **Accept all** or **Discard all**, or a change card's own **Accept** and **Discard**.
- **Auto** saves the assistant's changes at once, for everyone. **Undo run** reverts every save that reply made.

The assistant cannot accept its own drafts, lock a cohort, settle a decision, change members, or conclude the investigation. It proposes these steps for you to confirm.

## Decisions and proposals

The **Open decisions** list holds everything waiting for a person: decision records, the assistant's proposals, and your open drafts.

- **Open a decision** starts a decision record. Give the decision, its context, the options considered, and the plan sections it affects.
- To settle a record, pick an option, say why, and press **Accept**. **Reject** closes it without a choice. Accepted records appear under **Decisions in force**.
- A proposal is an action the assistant offers. Answer it with **Accept**, **Edit**, **Discuss**, or **Decline**.

## Findings

A finding is a conclusion in your own words that cites one exact result.

1. On a query card, choose **Record a finding…** in the ⋯ menu.
2. Under **Your finding**, state your conclusion in one or two sentences.
3. Press **Record**. If the result has changed, the portal asks you to read the new result first.

The **Findings** card lists every finding. **Withdraw** takes one back, and it stays listed as withdrawn.

## Cohorts and analysis releases

An analysis release is a fixed package of data released for analysis. It never changes, so a saved result stays the same. Every segment and query is saved for one analysis release.

**Lock as cohort…**, in a segment's ⋯ menu, turns one saved revision of the segment into a cohort in the Cohort Builder. The cohort is private to you, and the usual cohort rules govern it from then on. Later edits to the segment do not change the cohort. **Open the cohort** opens it in the Cohort Builder.

When a newer analysis release exists, a segment's ⋯ menu offers **Rebuild for** that release. A rebuild moves the segment and everything that depends on it together.

## Members

Press **Members** in the header to see everyone in the investigation. The owner adds and removes members. Members work on the content as the owner does. Only the owner renames, concludes, reopens, or archives the investigation. Nobody outside the members can open it.

## Notebooks

**New notebook** asks for a few words about the notebook. **Add and open** opens it in JupyterLab in your own workspace. Your changes reach the investigation only when you press **Commit my changes**.

## Snapshots, citation, and download

A snapshot is a fixed copy of the whole investigation at one moment. It never changes and is never deleted.

- **Take snapshot**, on the **Snapshots** card, takes one.
- **Copy citation** copies a citation for a paper. **Copy CFF** copies it in Citation File Format.
- **Download** saves the whole history as a git bundle. The card shows the command that opens it.
- **Link a publication**, on the **Publications** card, records a paper that came from the investigation.

## Concluding, reopening, and archiving

Only the owner uses the **Outcome** card.

- **Conclude** records how the work ended: **Answered**, **Not answerable with portal data**, **Moved to enclave analysis**, **Superseded by another investigation**, or **Stopped**. It takes a snapshot and makes the investigation read-only.
- **Reopen** makes a concluded investigation active again.
- **Archive** makes it read-only for everyone and moves it to the Archived list. **Unarchive** brings it back.
